# 3D route

3D uses three.js in the same render pipeline. It is optional and the less proven route.

- **Cost:** about 2.2 s per frame per process on 2 cores, so 30 s takes over half an hour, against about 4 minutes in 2D. Tell the person before starting. Keep 3D films short on the free plan, where it is untested.
- **Look:** it must look like the flat 2D style, not like a game. Use plain colours, the same dusk palette, soft light, a low rim of about .16, and simple shapes. If a still looks glossy or detailed, push it back toward 2D.
- **Setup:** `new_film.py film --3d` also installs three.js with npm. Node is needed. `render` uses software WebGL flags automatically.
- **Character:** `Person` in `lib3d.js`. Set `pos, yaw, feet, hands, pole, face` per frame. Use `gait()` for planted feet.
- **One render process:** 3D uses one process by default. Each shot is still its own file, so a stopped render resumes.

Lessons:
- Hang spot lights high.
- Choose a gun hand from the camera side. A character facing -x has their left on +z.
- Under a hat brim, keep head pitch small or the reaction is lost.
- Check bullet nose direction on stills.
- Lower lights during slow motion.
- Scene coherence comes free in 3D, but keep the same fixed positions.
